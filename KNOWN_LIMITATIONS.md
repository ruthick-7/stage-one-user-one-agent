# Known limitations

This is a working local baseline with explicit scope, not a complete production delivery platform.

1. **Home pages only.** Push, WhatsApp and in-app generation/scheduling are not implemented. No outbound messages exist to cancel after purchase. Messaging verifier tests demonstrate core restrictions but are not a production messaging certification. In particular free-form price/offer validation needs a structured renderer before enabling messages.
2. **Withdrawal revocation.** Future publications exclude withdrawn titles. Affected live pages are identified in audits, but there is no platform API to revoke or rebuild a page already published during its 120-minute TTL. The supplied simulator ignores TTL revocation and only validates at publication. This is a remaining requirement for a real deployment.
3. **Mock provider and degraded store.** All inference is deterministic/local. The provider adapter supports configured mock faults; no hosted adapter, retry backoff or external database is implemented. Store-degradation fixtures activate a conservative whole-window fail-fast circuit rather than reproducing the exact 20% timeout distribution. Actual SQLite writes are expected to work; unexpected disk/write failures fail the run instead of falsely reporting a durable decision.
4. **Scale and latency.** Replay is sequential with real local processing measurements and accelerated event times. It does not prove 1,000 concurrent requests/minute or real provider p95. Call budgets count logical reads/planning tools; raw SQL and verifier internal operations are not separate tools. Initialization and output reporting are outside per-trigger latency.
5. **Limited evidence.** Thirty days cannot prove lifetime "never watched/paid". Those goals carry explicit observation-window assumptions. Signup receipt is an approximation of signup time when devices buffer it. A 48h subscription freshness threshold and G06's 30-day lapse interpretation are conservative policy choices, not pack-specified constants. A changing device clock between creation and send remains uncertain.
6. **Undefined and absent outcomes.** G10 has no measurable predicate; G11's TV signal is absent. Neither is selected. They need product/instrumentation decisions rather than invented signals.
7. **Measurement coverage.** Simulator follow-up is restricted to triggered users. Full-arm recorded-completion ITT treats no recorded completion as zero and cannot estimate actual viewing among unobserved users. By-goal groups are descriptive baseline subgroups, sometimes tiny, and may have censoring. One seed and confidence intervals do not include simulator uncertainty. No headline rollout claim is warranted.
8. **Crash recovery scope.** Trigger idempotency survives process restart against SQLite; full timeline/control cursor resume is not implemented. CLI requests a fresh output directory. Initial decision/action writes are atomic, but measured timestamp refinement uses a second transaction; a crash between them leaves the earlier timestamp. A production outbox needs an explicit committed/ready/sent state and external idempotent publisher.
9. **Learning.** Outcomes and a conservative interpretation are stored, but no automatic policy optimizer learns from success rates. A goal may be attempted again in a new instance after an observation-window failure. This should become an explicit retry/cooldown policy before production.
10. **Packaging and privacy.** Provided data and full sample outputs are local and Git-ignored. Share them only through the assignment's authorized submission mechanism. A new checkout needs the supplied pack; no personal data is embedded in code. The supplied simulator fixes the practice day's date internally; unseen-day evaluation needs the evaluator's matching simulator or a separately authorized simulator change.
